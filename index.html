Visakhapatnam

India's largest AI hub outside the US

Google is spending roughly $15 billion through 2030 on three linked data-centre campuses around Vizag, aiming for 1 gigawatt of AI compute capacity. The build includes a new international subsea cable gateway, put together with AdaniConneX and Airtel, and it's one part of a much larger 5 GW pledge the state has made to Google, Meta and others combined.

  • 3 linked campuses
  • 1 GW planned capacity
  • 2028 commissioning target

Amaravati

A capital being built from open farmland

Confirmed as Andhra Pradesh's sole capital by an Act of Parliament in 2026, Amaravati is a ₹65,000 crore greenfield city on the Krishna river, master-planned for 3.5 million people by 2050. Campuses from VIT, SRM, Amrita and soon BITS Pilani are already landing alongside AIIMS Mangalagiri, backed by a World Bank and ADB financing package aimed at jobs in construction, manufacturing and services.

  • 217 km² masterplan area
  • 3.5M residents by 2050
  • 50K jobs targeted, phase one

Why Andhra Pradesh is building two engines at once

Since the 2014 split from Telangana left it without a capital, Andhra Pradesh has been running two development stories in parallel. Visakhapatnam is being pitched to global technology firms as a coastal, cable-connected base for AI compute — the kind of project that shows up fast, in dollars and megawatts. Amaravati is the slower, structural bet: a planned capital meant to anchor governance, education and administration for decades to come.

Neither is finished. Vizag still needs a power grid built out to match its ambitions, and Amaravati's Secretariat and High Court are still years from completion. But together they're the clearest signal of where the state expects its economy — and its graduates — to go next.

A state government official described the goal as building Andhra Pradesh into a two-trillion-dollar-class economy by 2047, with these two cities as its main engines.
